Theory, equations & model assumptions

Converter equations (per unit, 12-pulse pole)

The rectifier and inverter are represented by their average DC voltages. With \(R_c = d_x V_{do}\) the equivalent commutation resistance (\(R_c = \tfrac{3}{\pi}X_c\) per 6-pulse bridge):

\[ V_{dr} = k_r V_{do}\cos\alpha - R_{c} I_d, \qquad V_{di} = k_i V_{do}\cos\beta + R_{c} I_d = k_i V_{do}\cos\gamma - R_c I_d \] \[ L\,\frac{dI_d}{dt} = V_{dr} - V_{di} - R_d I_d \]

\(k_r, k_i\) are the AC commutation-voltage factors (1.0 = healthy; a 20 % dip gives 0.8). \(V_{do}\) is chosen so that the inverter delivers 1.0 pu at rated current with \(\gamma = \gamma_{ref}\).

Rectifier current controller (CC)

\[ \alpha_{ord} = \frac{\pi}{2} - \Big[K_p\,e + \frac{K_p}{T_i}\!\int e\,dt\Big], \qquad e = I_{ord}-I_d, \qquad \alpha_{min}\le\alpha_{ord}\le\alpha_{max} \]

Conditional integration prevents wind-up on the limits. A dead time \(T_d \approx 1/(2pf)\) (≈0.83 ms for a 12-pulse bridge at 50 Hz) represents firing-instant sampling.

Inverter: predictive CEA + current control with margin

\[ \cos\beta_{ord} = \cos(\gamma_{ref} + \Delta\gamma_{CC} + \Delta\gamma_{CFP}) - \frac{2 d_x I_d}{\hat k_i} \]

\(\Delta\gamma_{CC}\ge 0\) is the output of the inverter current controller, which acts only when \(I_d < I_{ord} - \Delta I\). If the rectifier is pushed to \(\alpha_{min}\) (weak rectifier AC voltage), current drops by the margin \(\Delta I\) and the inverter takes over current control — the classical mode shift. \(\hat k_i\) is the filtered inverter AC voltage; the filter lag is why sudden dips erode γ before the prediction catches up.

VDCOL

\[ I_{lim}(V_d) = \begin{cases} I_{min}, & V_d \le V_{low} \\ I_{min} + (1-I_{min})\dfrac{V_d - V_{low}}{V_{high}-V_{low}}, & V_{low}\(V_d\) is filtered with a fast time constant when falling and a slow one when rising, so the limiter grabs quickly and releases gently.

Commutation failure & DC-line faults

Each step the actual extinction angle is \(\gamma = \arccos(\cos\beta + 2d_xI_d/k_i)\). If \(\gamma < \gamma_{min} = \omega t_q\) the outgoing valve cannot regain blocking capability: the bridge is short-circuited (\(V_{di}\approx 0\)) for about one cycle. A DC-line fault splits the line at location \(x\) through \(R_f\); after the travelling-wave protection detects it, the rectifier is force-retarded to ~140°, the arc deionises, and the pole restarts with a ramped current order. Permanent faults trigger repeated restarts and finally a pole block.

Limits of this model

  • Average-value converter model — no valve switching, harmonics, or DC-line distributed capacitance/resonance.
  • Ideal tap changers and AC sources behind the commutation reactance; no AC-system dynamics or PLL.
  • Use PSCAD/EMTDC, EMTP, OpenModelica or ParaEMT for detailed EMT validation.

Theory: linearised current loop

Linearising the rectifier equation around \(\alpha_0\) gives \(\Delta V_{dr} = -V_{do}\sin\alpha_0\,\Delta\alpha\). With the inverter on CEA, \(V_{di}\) falls with \(I_d\) (\(-R_c I_d\)), cancelling the rectifier's commutation resistance, so the effective loop resistance is only \(R_{eq} = R_d\). With a constant-β inverter it is \(R_d + 2R_c\).

\[ G(s) = \frac{V_{do}\sin\alpha_0\; e^{-sT_d}}{(R_{eq} + sL)(1+sT_m)}, \qquad C(s) = K_p\Big(1 + \frac{1}{sT_i}\Big), \qquad L_o(s) = C(s)G(s) \]

Phase margin \(PM = 180^\circ + \angle L_o(j\omega_c)\) at \(|L_o(j\omega_c)| = 1\); gain margin \(GM = -20\log_{10}|L_o(j\omega_{180})|\). The dead time contributes \(-\omega T_d\) of phase and sets the practical bandwidth ceiling.

Auto-tune

For a target crossover \(\omega_c\) and margin \(PM\): the PI must supply \(\varphi_{PI} = -180^\circ + PM - \angle G(j\omega_c)\). Then

\[ T_i = \frac{1}{\omega_c\tan(-\varphi_{PI})}, \qquad K_p = \frac{1}{|G(j\omega_c)|\sqrt{1 + (\omega_c T_i)^{-2}}} \]

Note the operating-point dependence: \(\sin\alpha_0\) is small at low α, so the plant gain (and crossover) changes with tap position and AC voltage — one reason real CC loops use gain scheduling or non-linear gain.

Theory: why a small dip fails a commutation

The commutation area (volt-seconds) needed to transfer \(I_d\) between valves grows with \(I_d\) and shrinks with the commutating voltage. With the firing angle β already issued, a disturbance changes γ according to

\[ \cos\gamma = \cos(\beta_0 + \varphi) + \frac{2\,d_x\,I_d'}{k}, \qquad \cos\beta_0 = \cos\gamma_0 - 2 d_x I_{d0} \]

where \(k = 1-\Delta U\) is the remaining voltage, \(\varphi\) the shift of the commutation-voltage zero crossing (negative = earlier, typical for single-phase faults) and \(I_d'\) the current during the first commutation (it rises because \(V_{di}\) collapses faster than the rectifier responds). Commutation fails when \(\gamma < \gamma_{min} = \omega t_q\), giving the critical dip

\[ \Delta U_{crit} = 1 - \frac{2 d_x I_d'}{\cos\gamma_{min} - \cos(\beta_0 + \varphi)} \]

Because \(\Delta\gamma \approx \Delta\beta\,\sin\beta/\sin\gamma\), a few degrees of phase shift remove far more than a few degrees of γ — which is why asymmetrical faults are so effective at causing CF and why CF-prevention logic reacts to zero-sequence voltage as well as to magnitude.

Theory: converter Q, filters and switching logic

A line-commutated converter always absorbs reactive power. With overlap μ:

\[ \cos(\alpha+\mu) = \cos\alpha - \frac{2 d_x I_d}{U}, \qquad \frac{Q}{P} = \frac{2\mu + \sin 2\alpha - \sin 2(\alpha+\mu)}{\cos 2\alpha - \cos 2(\alpha+\mu)} \]

(use γ in place of α for an inverter). Typically \(Q \approx 0.5\text{–}0.6\,P\) at rated load. Switched AC filter / shunt-capacitor banks supply \(Q_f = n\,Q_b\,U^2\). On a Thevenin AC system with short-circuit ratio SCR,

\[ U \approx U_0 + \frac{Q_f - Q_{conv}}{SCR}, \qquad \Delta U_{bank} \approx \frac{Q_b}{SCR} \]

Reactive power controller (RPC)

  • Minimum filter — banks required for harmonic performance are connected first, as a function of P (absolute/minimum filter schedule).
  • Q control — keep the exchange \(Q_{ex} = Q_f - Q_{conv}\) inside \(Q_{ref} \pm B\) (operator bias = \(Q_{ref}\)). The dead-band must exceed one bank, \(2B > Q_b\), otherwise switching in one bank pushes \(Q_{ex}\) straight past the opposite limit → hunting.
  • U control / U limits — voltage limits override Q control: switch in below \(U_{min}\), out above \(U_{max}\), never below the minimum-filter requirement.
  • A switching delay (typically 1–5 s) confirms the condition before each operation.

Theory: dq current loop and DC-voltage loop

In a frame aligned with the grid voltage (ideal PLL) the phase-reactor/transformer dynamics are

\[ \frac{X}{\omega_b}\frac{di_d}{dt} = v_{cd} - v_{gd} - R i_d + X i_q, \qquad \frac{X}{\omega_b}\frac{di_q}{dt} = v_{cq} - v_{gq} - R i_q - X i_d \]

The controller cancels the cross-coupling terms \(\pm X i\) (decoupling) and feeds forward \(v_g\). With internal-model-control tuning the closed current loop becomes first order with bandwidth \(\alpha_c\):

\[ K_p = \alpha_c \frac{X}{\omega_b}, \qquad K_i = \alpha_c R, \qquad \frac{i}{i^*} \approx \frac{1}{1 + s/\alpha_c} \]

The converter dead time (PWM + computation, ≈1.5 sampling periods) limits \(\alpha_c\). The DC-voltage station regulates the energy stored in the DC capacitance (energy constant H):

\[ 2H\,V_{dc}\frac{dV_{dc}}{dt} = P_{dc,in} - P_{ac}, \qquad K_{pv} = 4H\zeta\omega_v, \quad K_{iv} = 2H\omega_v^2 \]

Feeding forward the measured DC power drastically reduces the voltage excursion after a power step from the remote station.
